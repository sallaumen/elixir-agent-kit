# Frontend: ten paired diagnostic reviews, candidate repeats and controls

This is a fixed-arm, read-only review experiment, not a routing evaluation, an implementation trial, or a release sign-off. Both arms received exactly the same excerpts of private Phoenix source at the parent of a historical change, the same English brief and, for seven cases, the same screenshot of a local reduced reproduction with invented display data. A received no kit text; B additionally received the verbatim workflow and shared contract from kit revision `5f59112` (plus the motion reference for the motion pair). The contract is part of the treatment: a gain cannot be attributed to the individual guide alone.

Each answer came from a fresh ephemeral Codex CLI 0.162.1 session with GPT-6.1-Sol at medium effort. No session received the later fix, the evaluator's measurements, the expected answer, another answer, or application history. The source was provided in the prompt; plugins, host skill discovery and the shell were disabled. The examiner checked distinct session IDs, the private source/fix parentage, prompt and guide digests, and absence of tool calls in the scored logs. An exploratory run that loaded a machine-wide guide was excluded, and both arms were restarted. The two additional paired cases test the central responsibilities of density and navigation separately from their neighboring responsive cases.

The examiner fixed three case-specific criteria before running either arm. Fresh judge sessions saw the criteria, the two anonymous answers in randomized order, and the examiner's independent observations. A score of 0 means missing/wrong, 1 partial, and 2 specific and correct. Judges did not see the treatment key, but a workflow-shaped answer can reveal its origin. Credit is for diagnosis and a proposed verification where the brief calls for review. No answer earns credit for operating a connected application, implementing a fix, or actually using assistive technology.

## Independent observations

The independent checks used the configured headless WebKit in a single tab, against local HTML reproductions of the historical structure and representative CSS. They are deliberately **not** full-page renderings: fonts, surrounding components, real data and LiveView behavior can differ. Only the screenshots went to the reviewers; the measurements and accessibility trees were reserved for adjudication.

- **Agreement gate:** at 375 px, the accessibility tree contained two agreement checkboxes, two document links and one primary submit, with no alternative exit. Explicit keyboard activation checked both boxes; the primary could receive focus. That does not prove the server records consent or permits return. The historical gate logic established the redirect and the actual exempt exit.
- **Team row:** at 375 px, the name's available/natural widths were 53/169 px; action rectangles were 114 × 44 and 91 × 44 px, and the document did not overflow. At 1280 px the name had 855 px. This is evidence of a local allocation problem; the wide teacher roster was separately assessed from historical source alone.
- **Workspace rail:** at 375 px the scrollable content was 744 px wide, with zero initial scroll; the marked item's edges were at 635 and 728 px. At 1280 px the same item was wholly visible. Seven destinations were already present. A separate historical drawer supplied an independent URL/history case.
- **Responsive disclosure:** the summary opened with Enter at 375 px. At 1280 px a fresh closed disclosure reported a computed block body and a nonzero child rectangle, yet the save control failed hit-testing, programmatic focus and visibility and was absent from the accessibility tree. The same checks passed for the open control. Geometry alone would have produced the wrong verdict.
- **Row actions:** a focus attempt failed on the outer clickable div (`tabIndex = -1`); its nested link and button could each receive focus. The accessibility tree contained neither a row opener nor its name. The reproduction does not establish whether any production click fires multiple events.
- **Document field:** the input exposed two native labels (one empty) even though the tree showed a plausible textbox name. In the rejected sample it had neither an invalid-state attribute nor a reference to the visible error. No screen reader was used and no spoken output is claimed.
- **Shared type:** the six original component excerpts contained 15 `text-xs`, 8 `text-sm`, 3 `text-base`, two 10 px literals and one 9 px literal. These sample counts are distinct from the larger product inventory in its design notes; no consumer-page rendering was tested.
- **Held press:** with regular motion, the plain button's brightness was about 0.80 at the middle sample and returned to normal after the 200 ms animation while still held. The plain link remained unchanged. With reduced motion, the 0.01 ms button animation had ended by the second sample. Certain shared controls independently applied an active scale. These traces do not demonstrate remote success, keyboard equivalence or native-device behavior.

## Second accessibility candidate and navigation routing check

- **Accessibility, revised wording:** the original guided result scored 4/6 versus 5/6 for a fresh guided answer. The revised instruction asks the reviewer to trace both the rendered associations and every existing validation source. The answer identified both labels, kept ID/name/value handling, respected the field error and separate rejection paths, and traced recovery. It did not expressly retain numeric input mode.
- **Accessibility, inverse:** both the current guide and that exact revised guide scored 6/6 and 6/6 on the same historical shared component with one error and correct native associations. Neither invented an extra label or called for a semantic change. This is a different case from the original composed field, using real source and its existing component-test example, not an assistive technology run.
- **Navigation, neighbor:** the identical rail packet was also paired with the unmodified `responsive-layout` guide. The original navigation-guided answer scored 3/6, and the responsive-guided answer scored 4/6 in this comparison. The latter recognized the missing active-item reveal, retained route/history semantics, and proposed wrapping on phones rather than adding another disclosure. It consciously rejected scrolling as the sole solution. Wrapping is a defensible design, but neither fresh guided session tested the smaller intervention of revealing only an offscreen selection. Routing to the neighbor reduced the scope of the proposal; it did not prove the original narrow fix.

These candidates were written after observing misses and rerun on the same case, which favors them. Only the revised accessibility candidate also has a no-change control. The historical fixes are comparators; an alternative design need not match them to be useful. The public results exclude raw source, screenshots, account data, private revisions and executor transcripts.

## Decision after the initial round

Here “supported” means that the guided answer diagnosed the essential defect and proposed a plausible verification on the named read-only case. It is not evidence that frontline agents selected the workflow on their own or implemented it successfully. Neither a completed pair nor a point total automatically clears a release gate.

| Workflow | Status for these diagnostic cases | Limits and next decision |
|---|---|---|
| `ui-identity` | **Pending** | The baseline missed a real alternative to a blocking primary action. A candidate recovered the candidate exit but did not fully establish its destination or improve the signature/clause hierarchy. Keep it experimental until an independent gate is tested. |
| `layout-density` | Supported on the phone boundary and wide roster | Both arms protected identity and hit-area allocation. The guide added desktop-state checks. No fixed application layout was rendered. |
| `navigation-ux` | Supported on drawer history; **pending at the width boundary** | Both arms solved history provenance, direct-entry fallback and Back. Navigation-guided rail advice added a disclosure; responsive advice was smaller, but neither tested visibility of the existing current item before restructuring. |
| `responsive-layout` | Supported on the disclosure | Matches independently measured native behavior; implementation and connected width transitions were not tested by executors. |

## Independent candidate checks for the three remaining concerns

Each proposed addition below was frozen *before* the original case was repeated and the separate control was run. On the original case the candidate got one fresh run against the stored baseline guided answer, with the exact same source, screenshot, brief and contract. On each independent control both baseline and candidate got fresh runs with identical inputs. The full prompts differed only by one general decision point. Two new judging sessions saw the randomized answers, the three preregistered criteria per case and independent observations, without the treatment key. This adds nine executor sessions and two judges, for **35 distinct executor sessions and eight judges** overall. The original session word limit remained 500.

| Guide candidate | Original case, current → candidate / 6 | Independent case, current → candidate / 6 | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `ui-identity`: trace the choices and their consequences for each arriving person | 3 → 5 | 2 → 2 | The mandatory gate regained a real exit and the redirect explanation. The separate program/individual-session review did not show reliable transfer. **Defer this guide change.** |
| `navigation-ux`: distinguish route state, local-clipping and page-width failure before changing navigation | 2 → 6 | 5 → 4 | The first answer now kept the existing rail, focus and page scroll, revealing the active item only when needed. On an independently overflowing admin header, the candidate routed to responsive layout, did not prescribe scrolling and kept the commands. **Supported for these cases.** |
| `motion-design`: compare the computed held state with the animation, keeping meaningful displacement only | 4 → 6 | 5 → 6 | The candidate removed decorative scale while preserving stationary feedback. On a separate reorder gesture it kept the pointer-driven translation and distinguished the reduced-motion override from the normal tremor. **Supported for these cases.** |

The identity transfer case matters: the historical program could show a public sold-out warning to a person already holding a seat. A prominent action could commit the full package, while the individual-day path was less visible. The candidate described several visitor states but did not identify the simultaneous contradictory conditions or a confirmation that named the scope and price of the commitment. The earlier agreement rerun also did not fully distinguish signing from the hierarchy of its supporting clauses. We did not add wording about this particular product to force the answer.

The navigation control proposed wrapping the header instead of compressing the duplicated branding first. Its score fell one point versus the original guide because it omitted a direct-entry verification on this width-only control; both answers got full credit for distinguishing whole-bar overflow from an offscreen selection. It was useful for distinguishing page overflow from a local rail, not proof that the candidate always chooses the smallest visual change. The motion control kept the correctly reduced drag translation, even though the candidate also recommended removing the decorative tremor in the normal branch; that aesthetic recommendation was not independently validated.

Further examiner checks, using the original historical rail hook and applicable CSS on reduced local fixtures:

- At 375 px the menu held 744 px of content, but the document itself fitted. The original hook took the menu from scroll offset 0 to 369 and made the current item fully visible. A manually chosen offset of 364 stayed 364 on another update while the item remained visible. Focus and document scroll were not moved by the hook; at 1280 px a fitting menu stayed at offset 0.
- After a 300 ms hold on a shared-button reproduction, the browser still computed scale **0.97** under reduced motion, even though animation duration had fallen to **0.01 ms** and the brightness flash had ended. Thus changing duration alone cannot deliver stationary pressed feedback for this case.
- On the separate drag fixture, the normal CSS produced a scale of about 1.055 with a rotating ghost. Reduced motion produced scale 1, rotation 0 and no animation, while retaining the same pointer displacement of **(36, 22) px**. Removing every transform would break that useful signal.

**Decision before the final holdout:** the revised accessibility, navigation and motion instructions have sufficient evidence for a narrow guide change. Keep the other five guides as tested. Of those five, **UI identity remains pending**: the separate transfer case failed an essential hierarchy/commitment check. Do not equate the seven case-supported workflows with validation of the full frontend catalog or with a release decision. These are bounded live diagnostic reviews, not client routing, before/after application implementation or a user study.

## Final UI identity holdout

One final pair tested a new, previously unseen payment-details screen. Before either answer, the examiner fixed three criteria: identify simultaneous coverage and settlement despite a primary payment link; preserve receipt/details access with an action weight grounded in whether money is due; and distinguish repeated or misleading paid/waived copy. The user brief asked for hierarchy and next steps and did not name the overlapping predicates. The added general instruction asks reviewers to examine independently true conditions together, including the commitment that remains for the same person. Neither that instruction nor the preceding candidate names the product. The original guide and final candidate scored **3/6 and 3/6** in a new blinded judging session.

The candidate explicitly combined package coverage with paid and waived states and kept a useful package link while questioning payment emphasis. The baseline also recognized the problem of a permanently primary link. The candidate did not fully establish all settled/refunding combinations or the standalone paid-versus-waived copy distinction. This is partial transfer to one independent case, not proof that the final wording resolves the earlier purchase-confirmation miss. No further wording or case was fit to these responses.

**Release interpretation:** accessibility, navigation and motion have supported, narrowly scoped guide changes. UI identity remains pending for the consent/commitment and overlapping-state family: the first novel transfer case missed the contradictory statuses and informed package confirmation, and this final comparison did not close those gaps across cases. Leave that experimental wording unpublished. Across the frontend experiments there were **37 distinct executor sessions and nine fresh judge sessions**, all read-only. No all-frontend release sign-off.
